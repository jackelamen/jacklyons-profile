Arriving in Seoul, and Never Quite Leaving

January 8, 2026 · 6 min read
This is an example journal entry, included to show the format. Replace this text — and everything below it — with your own writing.

I didn't plan on staying. That's the part people never believe when I tell it — that the move to Seoul in 2010 was supposed to be temporary, a detour before whatever came next. I don't think "next" ever showed up. Or maybe it did, quietly, somewhere between the third year and the fifth, and I just never noticed the hinge point.

There's a specific kind of disorientation that comes from building a life in a language you're still learning, in a city that doesn't slow down to wait for you. You get good at nodding along a half-second behind everyone else. You get good at reading rooms instead of menus. And at some point — I couldn't tell you exactly when — the disorientation stops feeling like a deficit and starts feeling like a skill.

What I remember most from that first year isn't any single event. It's the texture of it: humid August nights in Itaewon, learning which bartenders would let a broke DJ hang around past closing, the specific sound of Seoul at 4am when even the taxis have mostly given up. None of it looked like a plan. All of it turned out to be one.
